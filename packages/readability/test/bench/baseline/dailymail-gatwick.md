# All flights grounded at Gatwick airport with runway closed after plane suffers 'technical issue'

By DAVID OLASEINDE

Published: 14:39 BST, 4 September 2026 | Updated: 19:07 BST, 4 September 2026

Flights at [Gatwick](https://www.dailymail.com/news/gatwick-airport/index.html) Airport were halted for nearly an hour after a plane became stuck on the runway following a technical issue. 

A Boeing 787 aircraft became stuck on the runway due to a problem with its landing gear and had to be towed away. 

The plane appeared to be surrounded by several fire engines and the runway was closed for around 50 minutes.

The Daily Mail understands the affected flight was a Norse Atlantic Airways service to New York that was due to take off just after 1pm. 

Thousands of passengers faced travel disruption, as airport staff worked hard to resume flights as soon as possible. 

Gatwick has two runways but, due to their proximity, they can't be used at the same time. 

Passengers were advised to still travel to the airport and check online with their airlines.

One person posted on X: 'Scenes at Gatwick, Norse plane rejected takeoff (apparently stuck nose gear), stuck on runway with about 20 ground vehicles surrounding it. 

'Loads of aircraft holding, could be tonnes of diversions!'

Another passenger travelling from the Isle of Man added: 'I'm stuck on a plane returning from the IOM to Gatwick after jawbone surgery yesterday.. exhausted.

'The plane has diverted to Luton…I'd rather get out and catch a train back to London!'

A London Gatwick spokesman said: 'The runway was closed for 50 minutes this afternoon (Friday 4 September) due to a technical issue with a departing aircraft.

'The aircraft was towed from the runway, which reopened shortly afterwards following a full safety inspection.

'As always, safety and security is our number one priority.'

A Norse Atlantic spokesman said: 'Norse Atlantic confirms that one of its aircraft rejected take-off at London Gatwick Airport on 4 September 2026 following an indication of a potential technical issue.

'The aircraft stopped safely and was subsequently towed from the runway. 

'Safety is always our highest priority. The incident is under investigation and we are cooperating with the relevant parties. No passengers or crew members were injured.' 

It is the latest issue to affect Gatwick after passengers were left without running water in July. 

Flyers were unable to flush toilets, wash their hands or refill water bottles for hours due to the outage.
